# Visual research: patient medical services

For a new patient product or major redesign, collect 12–20 current references across at least three sources, including two of Pinterest, Dribbble, Behance, and 花瓣. Include at least one real public healthcare design system or service standard; use 4–6 references for a bounded component.

Search specific journeys: `patient portal appointment booking`, `lab results patient experience`, `prescription medication web`, `family proxy healthcare`, `患者端 预约挂号 UI`, `检查报告 解读 界面`, `互联网医院 Web`. Prefer recent portfolio work, but use NHS/CMS or comparable production guidance to challenge decorative concepts.

Record URL, date if known, patient journey, transferable pattern, safety or privacy risk, and relevance. Compare identity context, next-step clarity, medical-result hierarchy, uncertainty, consent, proxy switching, plain language, and error recovery.

Synthesize rather than imitate. Never copy brand identity, medical illustrations, proprietary assets, clinical wording, or a complete layout. Portfolio popularity is not evidence of clinical safety.

Reject AI-default healthcare signals: generic doctor hero images, blue-green gradients without purpose, oversized “health scores,” smiling avatar cards, ambiguous AI diagnosis, color-only abnormal values, excessive soft pills, fake patient data, and wellness decoration inside serious care flows.

If a platform is blocked, use indexed previews or another source without bypassing access controls. If browsing is unavailable or forbidden, use supplied evidence and disclose that current trend research was not completed.
